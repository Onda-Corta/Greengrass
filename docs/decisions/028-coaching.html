# ADR-028: Coaching

Status: Proposed Date: 2026-11-25 Updated: 2026-11-26 — the ADR-018 review answered the twelve questions this ADR sent. The bounded coach has none of the four gated properties and may ship; briefings, recommended actions, acting from a coach and reading records are permitted under the posture and remain unspecified product work. The answers are in § The review's answers. Status unchanged: who may write a coach, and who maintains the organizing knowledge, are still open. Sources: spec/coaching.md, spec/users.md, spec/support.md, spec/comms-intelligence.md, design/architecture/system.md, decisions/005-offline-first-sync.md, decisions/013-analytics-ai.md, decisions/014-volunteer-onboarding.md, decisions/016-cross-cutting-resolutions.md, decisions/017-sharing-contract-trust-model.md, decisions/018-ai-agent-posture.md, decisions/019-central-services-and-metered-billing.md, decisions/020-central-service-line-up-and-builders.md, decisions/022-core-modules-and-lanes.md, decisions/023-surfaces-chat-admin-field.md, decisions/026-two-lane-pricing.md

# Context

On 2026-11-24 the project was shown the sandbox of a distributed-organizing product. It gives every role an AI coach. An organization-management coach sits at the top of the administrator's home page, under the question "What do you need to get done today?", with suggestions such as A group is stuck and Announcement talking points. A group-leader coach answers a leader who describes a member coming to them privately, and explains it with a named author's idea about how groups form. The organization can write coaches of its own. A context layer the organization sets (mission, brand, guardrails) informs every coach. Around the coaches sit a daily briefing, a ranked list of recommended actions with Add task and Reach out buttons, voice input, past conversations, a share button and a notice that coaches use AI and can make mistakes.

The corpus has decided AI five times (ADR-018 § Context). Two of those decisions are close to a coach and are not one. The AI concierge (ADR-014) helps with the platform and is grounded strictly in the knowledge base. A text builder (ADR-020) turns a brief into a draft. Nothing in the corpus helps a person think through their own work: how to run a meeting, what to do about a group that has stalled, what to say in an announcement.

The chat of ADR-023 is the closest thing. A coach shares its shape, a conversation with a model, and much of the sandbox's coaching needs exactly what the chat needs: reading across features, acting on records, speaking first. That is the agent the ADR-018 review exists to settle.

This ADR is Proposed. Two things in it are decided, and are marked.

# Conflicts this ADR resolves

  1. ADR-022 § What the Core contains lists no coaching. This ADR adds it, as decided. The Core must stay lane-neutral, and coaching is: what a coach knows about organizing is the client's context and the lane's configuration, not the Core's.
  2. system.md § AI concierge grounds the concierge strictly in the knowledge base and platform documentation, with "no broader reasoning or external knowledge". The coach in the sandbox draws on general knowledge of how groups work. The concierge's rule stands as written, for the concierge. The coach's grounding is proposed below and is a different rule.
  3. users.md § Volunteer Team Lead is a field-level role for an operation: a canvassing day, a phone bank. The sandbox's group leader runs a standing local group over months. No role in the corpus describes that. Named, not resolved: this ADR gives coaches to the roles that exist and adds no role.
  4. ADR-018 § No agent capability reaches production until this ADR is accepted gates three properties the sandbox's coaching has. The briefing and the recommendations read groups, contact history and setup status: read scope wider than one feature. Add task and Reach out act: tool authority. The briefing appears before anyone asks: autonomous action. The gate stands. This ADR splits coaching into a bounded coach, drawn to have none of the four properties, and the rest, which waits.
  5. ADR-016 §49 commits that "staff can see exactly why a suggestion was made", and made reallocation suggestions rule-based for v1 to keep that promise. A model's recommended actions cannot keep it as written. Named, and sent to the review with the recommendations.

# Decision

# Coaching is part of the Core

Decided. Every instance has Coaching, in both lanes, the way every instance has the CRM.

The reason is the agent posture. Coaching is where most people will meet whatever ADR-018 settles: read scope, tool authority, autonomy, audit. If coaching were a module, each client that enabled it would carry its own version of those rules, and the rules could drift between what the chat does and what a coach does. In the Core, coaching is built on the same posture as the chat and changes when the posture changes. It is the CRM's reasoning applied to AI: one place where the rule holds, so it cannot be honoured in one place and ignored in another.

What follows from that:

  • The Core never depends on a module (ADR-022). If a coach is ever allowed to read a module's data, it reads it through operations the module declares in its manifest, and only when the module is enabled.
  • Being in the Core does not move coaching past the gate. The ADR-018 gate binds the whole Core (ADR-023 § The ADR-018 gate binds the whole Core). What may ship before the review is only what has none of the four gated properties.
  • The cost is metered. Every turn is an inference call, metered under ADR-019 and priced by each lane's rule (ADR-026), or billed to the client by its own provider under BYOM (ADR-016 §38).

Alternatives considered: Coaching as a module in the catalogue was rejected for the reason above: a module that is in practice an agent would restate the agent posture per client. Coaching as a political-lane module was rejected because the mechanism is lane-neutral. A commercial client's account teams have roles and craft too. Folding coaches into the chat with no separate concept was not decided either way, and is sent to the review (question 12 below).

# The name

Decided. The capability is Coaching and each assistant is a Coach, in English and in Spanish. In Spanish both words stay as they are: el coach, los coaches, el Coaching. The sandbox's "eCoach" belongs to another product and is not used. Neither are asistente, which the glossary gives to wizard, agente, which is the AI agent ADR-018 gates, or concierge, which is support's.

# What a coach is

Proposed.

A coach is a conversation with a model, set up for one role or one purpose, that helps a person with their own work. It advises, explains and drafts. It is not one of the three AI features it sits beside:

AI concierge Text builder Chat Coach
For Help with the platform A draft from a brief Doing the work Thinking through the work
Reads The knowledge base and platform documentation What the person hands it What the person may read, when they ask The conversation, attachments, and the context the organization designates
Acts No; escalates to a person No; returns a draft Yes; every write confirmed No (the bounded coach)
Standing Accepted (ADR-014) Accepted (ADR-020) Proposed and gated (ADR-023) Proposed (this ADR)

There are two kinds:

  • Role coaches. One for each role template in users.md § Permissions Model except Supporter, so for the Org Admin, each staff template, the Volunteer, the Volunteer Team Lead and the Candidate. The lane supplies their starting instructions; the commercial lane supplies its own roles.
  • Coaches the organization writes. A name, a purpose, instructions, the context documents it may use, and the roles that may open it. Who may write one is open.

# The bounded coach

Proposed. These are the bounds of ADR-020 § Builders, applied to a conversation.

  • A person opens it. A coach never speaks first and never runs in the background.
  • It reads three things: the conversation, what the person attaches, and the context documents the organization designated for that coach. It has no read path into contacts, voter records, groups, messages, donations, events, audit data or compartments.
  • It returns text. It invokes no platform operation. A draft leaves the conversation only when the person copies it somewhere, and once there it enters whatever approval that kind of content already has (ADR-021).
  • It holds one credential: to the model provider, under the client's entitlement or its own BYOM provider.
  • It keeps ADR-013's guardrails. It states no fact, figure or claim about the organization that is not in the conversation or the context.
  • It says what it rests on. A statement taken from a context document cites the document. An idea taken from a published author names the author and the work. Everything else is offered as the coach's own advice. This adapts comms-intelligence.md § 8.2 Provenance is mandatory to advice, where the rule there is written for findings.
  • It needs a connection. No field task depends on a coach (ADR-005, ADR-023).
Gated property The bounded coach
Read scope The conversation, attachments and designated context documents. Whether designated documents count as "wider than a single feature" is question 1 below
Tool authority None
Autonomous action None
Credential breadth The model provider only

A coach with no context documents reads only what the person hands it, which is a text builder's read scope exactly. A coach with context documents waited on question 1. The review answered it: designated context is not read scope wider than a single feature, because designation is a deliberate, logged tenant act (§ The review's answers). The bounded coach, with or without context documents, has none of the four gated properties and may ship.

# The organization's context layer

Proposed.

The organization writes once what every coach builds on:

  • Mission and voice: what the organization is for and how it speaks.
  • Guardrails: topics a coach declines, positions it never takes, language it avoids.
  • Context documents: items from the content store (ADR-021) the organization designates, such as its strategy, its training materials (support.md § In-Platform Training Module) and its knowledge base. Each coach uses the documents designated for it.

The layer lives in the instance and never crosses to another. A document held under a sharing contract cannot be designated, because the platform is never a party to a contract (ADR-017). Changes to the layer are logged with the person who made them. The lane supplies defaults.

The context layer is what ADR-018's harness drawing calls Strategy and Knowledge base (ADR-018 § The per-tenant harness, as drawn), specified for coaches. That makes it the harness's input, not the harness, and leaves the harness's other two sources, geo data and the CRM, out.

# What waits on the ADR-018 review

Answered 2026-11-26: the review permits each of these under the posture — see § The review's answers. None of it is yet specified for production; each needs its own specification before it ships. The section is kept as written, as the record of what was shown.

Recorded here so the review sees the whole of what was shown. None of it is specified for production, and nothing may be built on it as if it were.

  1. Briefings. A daily summary of the person's scope: groups active, people due for contact, setup unfinished. It reads beyond the conversation, and it either speaks first or runs on a schedule a person set.
  2. Recommended actions. A ranked list such as "You have not spoken with a member in 54 days." It reads contact history, raises ADR-016 §49's explainability promise, and ranks identifiable people.
  3. Acting from a coach. Add task and Reach out. Under the chat-as-hands proposal these would be declared operations, each confirmed with a card.
  4. Reading records in the conversation. A leader asks about their group and the coach reads its roster and activity.
  5. Coaches in other channels. A coach in Telegram or Signal, which turns on the default chat channel (ADR-023 § Cost and channel).

Briefings and recommendations have a version outside the gate: counts and rules computed without a model and shown as an ordinary dashboard, as ADR-016 §49 did for reallocation. That version needs a specification of local groups that the corpus does not have (conflict 3).

# Questions sent to the ADR-018 review

Recorded in ADR-018 § Questions recorded by later ADRs. All twelve are answered in § The review's answers; the questions are kept here as asked.

  1. Designated context and read scope. Does reading the context documents an organization designated for a coach count as read scope wider than a single feature? This decides whether the bounded coach is outside the gate. Read scope.
  2. Records. May a coach read groups, members, contact history or the CRM, to brief, to recommend or to answer? If so, within the person's own scope, as the chat-as-hands proposal says, or narrower, source by source? Read scope; item 2.
  3. Talking about people. A leader describing a named member's behaviour, and a coach analysing that person's place in the group, is profiling an identifiable person on a political platform. Is that permitted, and on what basis? Item 2.
  4. Acting. Are Add task and Reach out writes confirmed with a card, or links into the admin UI that the person completes there? Tool authority; item 6.
  5. Briefings. Is a daily briefing a schedule a person set, which ADR-020 and ADR-023 already allow for sends, or a coach speaking first? Autonomous action; item 6.
  6. Retention and who can read a conversation. How long conversations are kept, under which tier of ADR-016 § 4; what the audit log records; whether anyone but the person can read them, including the Org Admin, given the no-superuser-read-path rule at comms-intelligence.md § 8.5 Access and retention; and what sharing a conversation does. Item 3.
  7. Prompt injection from attachments, from designated documents, and from the instructions of a coach the organization wrote. Item 1.
  8. Offline. What a person sees without a connection, given that the people who lead local groups work close to the field. Item 4.
  9. Cost. Who pays when a volunteer talks to a coach, and what the default tier includes. Item 5; ADR-026.
  10. Contracts. Whether a coach may ever read data held under a sharing contract, such as a coalition's. Proposed here: never designated as context. Item 7.
  11. The model provider. Whether a coach may use an external BYOM provider when conversations carry members' names, which then leave the encryption boundary (system.md § The harness and the model router). Item 8.
  12. Coaches and the chat. Is a coach the chat of ADR-023 with a role's instructions and no operations, or a surface of its own? This ties to the Campaign Agent recommendation in ADR-022 § The module catalogue, which is not decided. The chat-as-hands proposal.

# Open questions

These do not depend on the review. They are not settled here.

  1. Who may write a coach. The Org Admin only, staff, team leads, or an alliance or association for its members over the bus (ADR-024, itself proposed).
  2. Who writes and maintains the organizing knowledge coaches draw on. GreenGrass, the lane, the client or partners, and in which languages.

# The review's answers

The ADR-018 review of 2026-11-26 answered the twelve questions. Each answer; the mechanism behind it is in spec/harness.md.

  1. Designated context and read scope. Reading the context documents an organization designated for a coach is not read scope wider than a single feature. Designation is a deliberate, logged, auditable tenant act, naming exactly that corpus for exactly that coach. The bounded coach has none of the four gated properties and is outside the gate.
  2. Records. A coach with manifest-declared read operations reads within the invoking person's own scope, the same rule as the chat. The bounded coach stays record-free; adding record reads is a declared operation, not a new posture.
  3. Talking about people. Permitted within the person's own scope. It is advice to a person, not a decision about the subject — the platform makes no solely automated significant decisions (compliance.md § Automated processing and the harness). Provenance and the context layer's guardrails apply.
  4. Acting. Add task and Reach out are writes: declared operations, each confirmed with a card at the moment it happens.
  5. Briefings. A schedule a person set. The briefing reads designated sources and what the subscribing person may read, evaluated under their scope at preparation time; never compartmented data.
  6. Retention and who can read a conversation. Conversations are prompt and response bodies: the Operational tier of ADR-016 § 4 (90 days to 5 years, default 2), with call metadata kept indefinitely in the audit tier. A coach conversation is private to its person; the Org Admin sees metadata — counts, cost — never content, under the no-superuser-read-path rule. Sharing sends a read-only copy to the chosen recipients, logged.
  7. Prompt injection. Attachments are untrusted content: data, never instructions (security.md § Prompt injection). Designated documents and org-written coach instructions are trusted by designation, and designation changes are logged — which makes who may write a coach a security question, recorded in ADR-018 § The review.
  8. Offline. A coach needs a connection; the coach page says so and offers cached conversations. No field task depends on a coach.
  9. Cost. Metered to the client under ADR-019, priced by the lane's rule (ADR-026); under BYOM the client's own provider bills it. What the default tier includes stays open in ADR-026.
  10. Contracts. A coach reads data held under a sharing contract only under the invoking person's contract, never more than that person could read, and never in the background. The proposal here stands: a contract document is never designated as context.
  11. The model provider. Conversation content is personal data, and routes only per the tenant's data-class rules under spec/harness.md § The model router. A tenant that wants members' names never to leave its boundary routes that class to in-boundary endpoints only.
  12. Coaches and the chat. A coach is a harness persona: instructions, designated context and a declared operation set. The bounded coach is the zero-operation case; the chat is the general case. One harness, many personas (spec/harness.md § Personas).

# Consequences

Benefits:

  • Every person gets help with their own work, in their own language, without a trainer in the room
  • An organization writes its mission, voice and guardrails once, and every coach keeps to them
  • The agent posture applies to coaches and the chat from one place
  • The bounded coach is a concrete, small case for the review, with its four properties stated

Costs:

  • Most of what made the sandbox's coaching useful, the briefing and the recommendations, waits on the review
  • Every turn is inference, and coaching invites long conversations
  • Conversations will contain what people say about other people, before anyone has decided how long they are kept
  • Lane-neutral coaches need organizing knowledge that someone has to write, and no one is named yet

Constraints:

  • The bounded coach ships inside its bounds: it never speaks first, never acts and never reads records. Briefings, acting and record reads are permitted by the review under the posture — a schedule a person set, declared operations confirmed with a card, the invoking person's scope — and each is specified before it ships
  • No field task depends on a coach
  • A document under a sharing contract is never coach context
  • The concierge's grounding rule is unchanged
